Modelling agency fees & scams in India

01

Do modelling agencies charge fees?

A legitimate modelling agency’s business is representing talent to clients. In the usual arrangement, an agency earns when its talent is paid for assignments it secures — typically as a commission agreed in a written contract — rather than by charging people simply to join.

So the short answer: an up-front registration or joining fee is a warning sign, not a standard step. That doesn’t mean every request for money is a scam; some agencies may, for example, ask a signed model to contribute to specific costs, and photographers legitimately charge for shoots. The difference is in the conditions:

  • Is the payment a condition of being considered or “registered”? That’s the red flag.
  • Is the money going to a clearly identified business, against a written agreement and a proper invoice, for a specific service you chose? That’s a normal commercial transaction you can evaluate on its merits.

02

Red flags to watch for

  • “Pay to register” — a joining, registration, membership or profile fee before anything else happens.
  • A compulsory portfolio shoot — you’re told you must use their photographer, at their price, to be accepted.
  • Guaranteed work — promises of assured jobs, film roles or a fixed income. No honest business can guarantee that.
  • Urgency — “only two slots left”, “offer ends today”, pressure to pay before you can think.
  • Payment to a personal account — a personal UPI ID or bank account rather than a business one, with no invoice.
  • Escalating payments — a small fee first, then a “processing”, “audition” or “certificate” fee, then another.
  • Requests for revealing photos or video calls — especially early, from unverified accounts, or framed as a “test”.
  • Early requests for ID documents — Aadhaar, PAN or bank details before there’s any verified business relationship.
  • No verifiable work — no real past clients or campaigns you can trace, only stock-looking images or screenshots.
  • Celebrity name-dropping — claims of connections with famous directors or brands that you can’t verify independently.
  • Meetings in odd places — hotel rooms, private homes, or locations that change at the last minute.

If something feels wrong, it’s allowed to be wrong. You never owe anyone a payment, a photo or a meeting to “prove” you’re serious.

03

How to verify an agency or casting call

  1. Check the business is real. Ask for the registered company name. In India you can look up companies on the Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA) website, and a GSTIN can be checked on the official GST portal. A real business won’t mind you checking.
  2. Trace their work. Ask which campaigns they’ve cast and find those campaigns yourself — on the brand’s own channels, not just the agency’s screenshots.
  3. Talk to people they represent. Ask to speak to talent they work with, and look for independent mentions rather than only the reviews they show you.
  4. Check the contact details. A consistent website, business email address and a physical address that matches what they tell you. Be wary of contact only through a freshly created social account.
  5. Read the contract before signing. Duration, exclusivity, commission, image rights, how you can leave. Take it home; ask someone you trust to read it too.
  6. Go with someone. For any in-person meeting or shoot, take a friend or family member, and make sure it’s at a proper studio or public place.
  7. Pay only against an invoice, to a business account, for a service you’ve chosen — never as a condition of being considered.

If you’ve been scammed

Stop paying, keep every message, screenshot and payment receipt, and report it. In India you can report cyber fraud on the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal (cybercrime.gov.in) or call the national cyber crime helpline, 1930, and you can file a complaint with your local police. If revealing images are being misused, report the account to the platform as well. Acting quickly, especially on payments, gives you the best chance.

04

Staying safe at castings and shoots

Most of the people you’ll meet in this industry are professionals doing honest work. A few simple habits protect you from the ones who aren’t, and no genuine client will mind any of them:

  • Get the brief in writing first — the client, the date, the location, the outfits involved, and whether the shoot is paid or collaborative.
  • Agree the clothing beforehand. Swimwear, lingerie or anything revealing should be stated in the brief and agreed by you in advance — never sprung on you at the studio.
  • Bring someone. A friend or family member is a normal presence at a shoot. If a team won’t allow it, treat that as your answer.
  • Share your location with someone you trust and tell them when you expect to finish.
  • Agree image usage. Who can use the photos, where and for how long — even on an unpaid test.
  • Keep your own copies of the brief, the agreement and any payment receipts.

Questions, answered straight

Do modelling agencies in India charge a registration fee?

An up-front registration or joining fee, charged as a condition of being considered, is a common warning sign. Legitimate agencies usually earn a commission on assignments they secure, under a written contract, not by charging people to join.

Is it a scam if an agency asks me to pay for a portfolio shoot?

It’s a red flag if the shoot is compulsory, with their photographer, as a condition of acceptance. Paying a photographer you chose, for a clearly defined shoot with an invoice, is a normal transaction.

How can I check if a modelling agency is genuine?

Ask for the registered company name and check it on the MCA website, verify any GSTIN on the GST portal, trace the campaigns they claim, speak to talent they represent, read the contract in full and never pay to a personal account.

Where do I report a modelling scam in India?

Report it on the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal (cybercrime.gov.in) or call the 1930 helpline, and file a complaint with your local police. Keep all messages and payment records.
