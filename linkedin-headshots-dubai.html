A LinkedIn headshot is a different job from a portrait, and the difference is the frame. A portrait is made to be looked at. A profile photo is made to be recognised — small, circular, next to your name, usually while someone is scrolling past. Those are not the same picture, and shooting one when you needed the other is why so many people quietly dislike their own profile.

The practical consequences are specific. A wide, elegant crop makes your face tiny once it is reduced to a circle. A busy background turns to noise. A dramatic shadow down one side reads as a smudge rather than as mood. A tilted head that looks relaxed at full size looks off-balance beside a column of centred faces. None of this is about the camera — it is about knowing where the picture is going before you take it.

So that is how I shoot it. Tighter framing than a standard portrait. A background quiet enough to survive being shrunk. Even light on both sides of the face. And before you leave, we look at the frames at the size they will really appear, not blown up on a laptop — because that is the only honest test.

04

Why the directing matters more than the camera

Nearly everyone tightens up in the first two minutes in front of a lens, and a tightened face is exactly what reads as untrustworthy at thumbnail size. The eyes go flat, the smile arrives half a second late, the shoulders climb. No lens fixes that.

What fixes it is being directed by someone who does this on film sets, where the job is getting a real expression out of a person on a schedule. I talk you through posture and angle, give you something to react to rather than telling you to smile, and shoot through the moment rather than at it. That is why people who say they hate photographs tend to leave with one they will actually use.

The film background shows in the light as well. Even, soft light on both sides of the face is unglamorous and deeply practical: it survives being shrunk to a circle, and it will still look right in two years when the fashionable lighting of this year looks dated.

Common questions

How is a LinkedIn headshot different from a corporate headshot?

A LinkedIn headshot is built for one small circular frame, seen at thumbnail size, by someone deciding in about a second whether to open your profile. A corporate headshot is built for consistency across a team page. The lighting and direction overlap; the framing does not. For a LinkedIn photo I shoot tighter, keep the background quiet, and compose for a circle rather than a rectangle, so nothing important sits where the crop will cut it.

What should I wear for a LinkedIn photo in Dubai?

Wear what your clients would expect to see you in, one notch sharper. Solid mid-tones photograph best; tight patterns and thin stripes can shimmer at small sizes, and pure white or pure black can flatten. Bring two options and we will shoot both, which costs nothing extra and gives you a choice once you see them on screen.

Where do we shoot — studio, office or outdoors?

All three work and each gives a different read. A clean studio backdrop is the safest and most versatile. Your own office puts you in your world and suits founders and client-facing roles. Outdoors in soft light reads warmer and less corporate. If you are unsure, we shoot a plain background first so you always have the safe frame, then try a second look.

How long does a LinkedIn headshot session take?

For one person, 30 to 45 minutes is enough for several usable looks. Most of that is spent getting you comfortable rather than shooting. If you only need one strong frame, it can be done faster, but the extra fifteen minutes is usually what separates a photo you tolerate from one you actually like.

How many photos do I get, and can I use them anywhere?

You get a small set of fully retouched images rather than a large gallery, cropped for a profile photo and for wider use such as a website bio, a speaker page or a press mention. They are yours to use across your own professional profiles without any extra licence.

How fast is delivery?

Retouched images are typically delivered within 2 to 3 days. Rush delivery is available when you need the photo for a launch, a new role or a conference listing.

Both. A single profile photo is its own job and this page is about that. If you need the whole team done in one visit and matched to each other, that is the corporate headshots service instead, and it is priced per session rather than per person.
