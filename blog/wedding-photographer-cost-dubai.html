How Much Does a Wedding Photographer Cost in Dubai?

Straight answer first: most couples in Dubai spend somewhere between AED 5,000 and AED 15,000 on wedding photography — but the real number depends on a few things worth understanding before you book. Here's the honest breakdown.

Updated June 2026 · by Yedukrishna Suresh

The typical ranges in 2026

Dubai is a wide market — there are students with a camera and there are international names flown in. For a skilled, established photographer, here's roughly where the market sits:

Typical wedding photography packages and price ranges in Dubai, 2026 (AED).
PackageTypical range (AED)Usually includes
Essential2,500 – 5,000A few hours / single event, edited highlights gallery
Full day5,000 – 12,000Full-day coverage, full edited gallery, online delivery
Premium12,000 – 30,000+Multi-day or multi-event, second shooter, album, photo + film

These are market ballparks, not fixed prices — every wedding is different, which is exactly why most good photographers quote per wedding rather than list a flat menu. My own wedding photography is quoted all-in per event, with no hidden extras.

What actually drives the price

  • Hours & number of events. A single ceremony costs far less than a three-day Indian celebration with mehndi, sangeet and reception.
  • Photo only, or photo + film. Adding a wedding film is the single biggest factor — and usually the most worth it.
  • Second shooter. Two angles on the big moments (one on the couple, one on reactions) adds cost but a lot of coverage.
  • Albums & prints. A physical album is an add-on most couples are glad they kept.
  • Destination & travel. Out-of-Dubai or out-of-UAE weddings add travel and accommodation.
  • Experience. A wedding happens once and never pauses — experience is what you're really paying for.

Photo, or photo and film?

This is the question that decides most of the budget. A photo freezes a moment; a film keeps the sound and motion — the vows in their voice, the speech that made everyone cry, the first dance. Booking both with one team is usually more cost-effective than two separate vendors, and it means a single, consistent look. If something has to flex, flex the album before you drop the film. (More on that on my wedding videographer page.)

What should be in the package

  • An agreed number of coverage hours and events
  • A full edited gallery (not just a handful of previews) with clear delivery timing
  • High-resolution downloads and print rights
  • A highlight selection for sharing quickly
  • If film is included: a highlight film, and ideally a same-day edit to play at the reception

How to choose (don't just pick the cheapest)

The cheapest quote is rarely the cheapest decision — you only get married once, and you can't re-shoot it. Before you book:

  • Watch a full gallery and a full film, not just a curated 6-second clip.
  • Check they've shot your kind of wedding — Indian, expat, destination — and know the rituals and pace.
  • Make sure the quote is all-in, so there are no surprise charges for editing, travel or extra hours.
  • Meet the actual person shooting it — chemistry matters when they're with you all day.

The short version

Budget around AED 5,000–15,000 for solid wedding photography in Dubai, more if you add film, a second shooter or multiple days — and treat it as one of the few wedding costs you'll still care about in ten years. Spend where it lasts.

Common questions

What is the average cost of a wedding photographer in Dubai?

As a rough market guide in 2026, wedding photography in Dubai typically runs from around AED 2,500–5,000 for short or single-event coverage, AED 5,000–12,000 for full-day coverage with edited galleries, and AED 12,000–30,000+ for premium or multi-day weddings and photo-plus-film packages. The right number depends on hours, the number of events and whether you add video.

Should I book photo and video together?

Usually yes. Booking photo and film with one team means a consistent look, no two crews blocking each other, and it's typically more cost-effective than hiring separately. A film captures the motion and sound a photo can't — the vows, the speeches, the first dance.
