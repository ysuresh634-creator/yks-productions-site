How Much Does a Real Estate Video Cost in Dubai?

Short answer: a single-property film in Dubai usually runs AED 1,500 to AED 8,000, with developer and villa packages higher. But the cheapest option is often the most expensive mistake — here's why, and what actually changes the price.

Updated June 2026 · by Yedukrishna Suresh

Ranges, not fixed prices — a studio apartment and a Palm villa are different jobs. Most good videographers quote per property, all-in. My real estate video is priced that way, with stills options from the same visit.

What drives the price

  • Property size & type. A one-bed apartment is a quick shoot; a multi-floor villa with grounds takes far longer.
  • Deliverables. One reel, or a hero film plus reels plus stills? Each format adds time in the edit.
  • Twilight & timing. Golden-hour and blue-hour exteriors are the highest-converting frames, and they take a planned, longer shoot.
  • Turnaround. Standard delivery vs a same-day rush for a hot listing.
  • Styling & access. Staging, building approvals and coordinating access all factor in.

Why the cheapest option costs you the listing

A phone walkthrough with a wide lens and a stock track is cheap because it's worth what it costs — it fills the portal's video slot but it doesn't sell. On Bayut and Property Finder, where buyers scroll dozens of near-identical Marina apartments, a flat video is invisible. A properly shot, graded and paced film is what makes a buyer stop, save and book a viewing. For a unit worth millions, saving a few thousand dirhams on the video that markets it is a strange place to economise.

Photos, video, or both?

Photos are non-negotiable — they're the thumbnail and the gallery. Video is what increasingly decides which listing gets the enquiry. The most cost-effective route is booking both from one visit so the look is consistent and you're not paying two crews. (See how I combine them on the real estate photography page.)

Note what is not on that list: aerial footage. Drone work is a separate specialism with its own licensing, and any property film that needs it should be quoted with a licensed operator brought in for that part. Treat a quote that casually promises aerials as a quote worth asking questions about.

What actually changes the price

  • The size of the unit. A one-bed apartment and a six-bedroom villa are not the same day, because coverage scales with rooms.
  • Whether it is furnished. An empty unit is faster to shoot and much harder to make feel like somewhere a person would live.
  • Access. A vacant unit with a key is straightforward. A tenanted one, or a building with restricted hours, turns a two-hour shoot into a half day.
  • How many units at once. The largest saving available — several units in the same tower on the same visit share all of the setup and travel.
  • Turnaround. A listing going live on Thursday is a different job from one going live next month.

Agents, developers and owners want different films

The same budget buys a different thing depending on who is spending it, and briefs go wrong when this is not said out loud.

An agent needs volume and speed — a consistent, repeatable piece per listing that goes up fast and looks the same every time. Consistency matters more than artistry, because the films are a system, not a one-off.

A developer is selling something that may not exist yet, so the film is doing brand work as much as property work: the lifestyle, the location, the finish quality. That justifies a bigger production and a longer edit.

An owner or a holiday-home host usually wants one strong film for a portal listing and social, and is best served by the simpler tier done properly rather than an ambitious one done thinly.

The short version

Budget AED 1,500–8,000 for a single Dubai property's video depending on size and deliverables, more for launches and villas — and remember the video isn't the cost, it's the marketing that moves the asset. Spend like the listing's worth it, because it is.

Common questions

How much does a real estate video cost in Dubai?

As a 2026 market guide, a vertical social walkthrough reel for a single unit runs around AED 1,500–3,500, a cinematic hero film with social cuts around AED 3,500–8,000, and a developer launch or show villa with multiple deliverables AED 8,000–25,000+. Unit size, whether it is furnished, access and turnaround are what move it within those ranges.

Is a property video worth it versus just photos?

For most listings the honest answer is that you want both, because they do different jobs: photographs are what a buyer scans, and video is what makes them feel the space and enquire. If the budget only stretches to one, photographs remain the baseline for a portal listing — video is what makes a listing stand out once the photographs are already good.

What is the cheapest way to get a property filmed properly?

Book several units in one visit. Setup and travel are the fixed costs and they are paid once per trip rather than once per property, so three units in the same tower cost dramatically less than three separate bookings. For an agency with regular stock this is the difference between video being an occasional treat and a standard part of every listing.

Does the price include drone or aerial footage?

No. Aerial work is a separate specialism with its own licensing requirements, and when a film genuinely needs it, it is arranged with a licensed operator and quoted separately. Most property films do not need it — a well-shot walkthrough sells a unit more reliably than an establishing shot from above.

How long does a property shoot take?

A single apartment is typically two to three hours on site, a large villa most of a day. Preparing the space is a real part of that time, and it is the part that most affects how the finished film looks.

How quickly can the video be delivered?

For a standard listing film, two to four days is normal, and faster is possible when a listing has a launch date. Agreeing the deliverable list before the shoot is what keeps a rush turnaround realistic.

Do I need the unit to be furnished or staged?

It helps considerably, but it is not essential. An empty unit shoots faster and can look clean and architectural; a furnished one gives a buyer something to imagine themselves inside. If a unit is empty and the budget allows any staging at all, a few pieces in the main living space earn their cost back on screen.

What deliverables should I ask for?

At minimum: a horizontal cut for the portal listing, a vertical cut for social, and a short teaser. Ask for the list to be written into the quote rather than assumed, because extra versions requested after the edit is finished are the most common source of an unexpected invoice.

Can you shoot photos and video in the same visit?

Yes, and it is usually the sensible way to do it — one visit, one preparation of the space, and stills and film that match each other because they were lit and framed by the same person on the same day. It also costs less than booking the two separately.
