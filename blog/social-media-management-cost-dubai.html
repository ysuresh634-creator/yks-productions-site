How Much Does Social Media Management Cost in Dubai?

Most Dubai brands pay between AED 3,000 and AED 15,000 a month — but the number that matters isn't the retainer, it's how much real, scroll-stopping content it actually buys. Here's the honest breakdown.

Updated June 2026 · by Yedukrishna Suresh

The typical ranges in 2026

Typical social media management retainers in Dubai, 2026 (AED per month).
LevelPer month (AED)Usually includes

Content-led5,000 – 12,000A monthly shoot, reels + photos, strategy, posting and reporting

Ranges, not fixed prices. The single biggest variable is how much original content — especially video — gets shot each month. That's also what actually drives results. My own social media service is content-led and quoted all-in per month.

Why the cheapest option rarely works

A AED 1,500/month "we'll post for you" package sounds great until you realise it's recycled stock and AI captions. Instagram and TikTok reward original, native, scroll-stopping content — overwhelmingly video now. If nobody's actually shooting for your brand, you're paying someone to keep a dead account warm. The money is better spent on fewer, better pieces of real content.

What actually drives the price

  • Content volume. How many reels and posts, and how often you shoot, is the main cost driver.
  • Shot, or just scheduled? A retainer that includes a monthly shoot costs more — and is worth far more.
  • Number of platforms. One channel done well beats five done badly; more channels means more cost.
  • Strategy & ads. Adding paid-ad management and reporting raises the retainer.
  • One operator vs an agency. A single creative who shoots and manages is usually leaner than an agency that outsources the camera.

The smart way to buy it

  • Ask "who actually shoots the content?" — if they outsource it, the quality will show.
  • Prioritise video — it's what the algorithm pushes.
  • Start focused — one or two platforms done brilliantly beats spreading thin.
  • Look for an all-in monthly number so there are no surprise charges.

What you are really buying

Social media management is sold as one thing and is actually two, and almost every disappointing retainer is a mismatch between them.

The two halves of a social retainer, and which one a given price is mostly paying for.
HalfWhat it coversWhat happens when it is missing
Making thingsShooting, editing, designing — original photos, reels and graphicsThe feed fills with stock, reposts and text-on-colour. Engagement falls and nobody can say why
Running thingsStrategy, calendar, captions, posting, replying, reportingGood content goes out at random, without a thread, and never compounds

The cheapest retainers are almost entirely the second half. That is not dishonest — scheduling and captions are real work — but if nobody is shooting anything, you are paying somebody to organise a shortage. Before comparing prices, ask each proposal how many original assets it includes per month. The answers vary far more than the fees do.

How to tell if it is working

Followers is the metric everyone reports and the one that matters least. What is worth watching instead:

  • Reach among people who do not follow you. If most of your views come from existing followers, the content is being served to the room rather than to the street.
  • Saves and shares, which say someone found it useful enough to keep or to pass on. These predict growth far better than likes.
  • Profile visits and link clicks, the closest thing to intent you get before an enquiry.
  • Enquiries that mention social, which requires asking every enquirer where they came from. Almost nobody does this, and it is the only number that touches revenue.

Agree which of those you are being judged on at the start. A retainer measured on follower count will produce content optimised for follower count, which is rarely the same content that produces customers.

Common questions

How much does social media management cost in Dubai?

As a 2026 market guide, a basic scheduling and captions retainer runs around AED 2,000–5,000 a month, a content-led retainer including a monthly shoot around AED 5,000–12,000, and full multi-platform management with heavy content volume and ads support AED 12,000–25,000+. The biggest single variable is how much original content is actually produced.

Is cheap social media management worth it?

It can be, if you already have content and only need it organised and posted. It is rarely worth it if you do not, because the cheapest retainers usually include little or no original shooting — which means paying someone to schedule a shortage rather than to solve one.

What should I ask before signing a retainer?

How many original assets are produced each month, who shoots them, how many are photos versus video, what the approval process looks like, and which metric you will be judged on. Proposals that are vague about the asset count are almost always light on production.

How many posts a month is right?

Fewer good ones beats more weak ones, and consistency beats volume. What matters more than the number is whether the content is original, because a feed of reposts and text graphics stops reaching new people regardless of how often it goes out.

What metrics actually matter?

Reach among non-followers, saves and shares, profile visits and link clicks — and above all, enquiries that mention social, which requires asking every enquirer where they found you. Follower count is the most reported metric and the least useful one.

How long before it works?

Expect two to three months before patterns are readable and meaningful change shows. Anything promising results in weeks is describing a spike rather than growth, and spikes do not compound.

Should content and ads be handled by the same person?

It helps considerably. Ads amplify content, so whoever is buying the media benefits from knowing what was shot and why — and when the two are split across agencies, the usual failure is a good budget spent on assets that were never made to be advertised.

Do we need to appear on camera ourselves?

Not always, but it is usually the cheapest advantage available to a small business. Faces outperform product shots consistently, and a founder who is willing to be on camera has an edge no budget can buy for a competitor who is not.

Can we start small and scale up?

Yes, and it is usually the sensible route. Start with a monthly shoot day and a modest posting cadence, find out what your audience responds to, and increase volume once you know which format is working rather than guessing at the outset.
