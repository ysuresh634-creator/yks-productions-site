An editorial is a set of pictures made to say something, not to sell a specific item. That sounds like a distinction for art directors, but it is really a commercial one: it changes what gets shot, what the day costs, and what the images are worth afterwards.

A lookbook answers a question — what does this garment look like. It is judged piece by piece, and its value is in clarity. An editorial answers a different one — what does this brand feel like this season. It is judged as a sequence, it needs an opening frame and a closing one, and its value is in the mood it establishes and the licence attached to it. One is priced on how many pieces get through the day. The other is priced on the production and the usage.

Getting these confused is the most expensive mistake in the category. A brand that briefs a lookbook and expects an editorial ends up with clean, well-lit pictures that say nothing. A brand that briefs an editorial and needs a catalogue ends up with eight beautiful frames and no product page.

03

Usage, and why it is in the quote

The question that separates a professional commission from an expensive misunderstanding is not what the day costs. It is where the pictures are allowed to go, and for how long.

The same shoot can be worth very different amounts depending on the answer. Images for a brand’s own social channels are one thing. The same images on a billboard on Sheikh Zayed Road, or running as paid media in another market, are another — not because the photography changed, but because the value the brand extracts from it did, and because the models were booked for one and not the other.

So it goes in the quote, in writing, before the shoot: which channels, which territories, how long. This protects you more than it protects me. A brand that discovers after the fact that its campaign imagery was never licensed for out-of-home has a real problem, and it is invariably discovered at the worst moment. Settling it at brief stage costs one sentence.

If you are not sure what usage you need, say so and we will work it out. The commonest honest answer is “our own channels for a year”, and that is a straightforward thing to quote.

07

Commissioning questions

What counts as an editorial shoot?

An editorial is a set of photographs made to tell a story rather than to sell a specific product. It runs as a feature in a magazine, as a brand's seasonal story, or as the imagery behind a campaign. The distinction that matters commercially is that an editorial is commissioned around an idea, where a lookbook or catalogue is commissioned around a rail of garments.

How is editorial priced differently from a catalogue shoot?

Catalogue work is priced on volume — how many pieces get through in a day. Editorial is priced on the production: how many looks, how many locations, what team is needed, and crucially what usage the images are licensed for. A single editorial image used in a paid campaign is worth more than fifty catalogue frames, and it is quoted that way.

What usage rights do I get?

Whatever is agreed and written into the quote before the shoot, rather than left vague. For most brand work that is full use across your own channels, website and paid social for an agreed period. Out-of-home, print advertising, or use in markets outside the UAE are quoted separately, because they change what the talent and the location are booked for, not just what I am paid.

Do you work to an art director's brief, or create the concept?

Both, regularly. If you have an art director and a deck, I shoot to it and my job is execution and problem-solving on the day. If you have a product and a season and no concept yet, I build the treatment — references, locations, casting direction and a shot list — and you approve it before anyone books anything.

Can you handle casting, styling and hair and makeup?

Yes, as part of the commission rather than as a separate hunt you run. Casting, styling, hair and makeup and location are all quoted inside the one figure. If you have an agency roster or a team you already work with, I work with them instead.

How far ahead do editorial shoots need to be booked?

Two to four weeks is comfortable for a single-location editorial with a small team, because casting and location are usually what set the pace rather than my availability. A campaign with talent booked through an agency, permits, or multiple locations needs longer.

What do we actually receive at the end?

A set of fully retouched images at the agreed count, in the crops you need — typically a horizontal and a vertical version of each hero frame, since the same story usually has to run on a page and on a phone. Editorial retouching is done frame by frame rather than batched, which is why the delivered count is deliberately small.

Do you shoot motion on the same day?

Yes, and for most brands it is the sensible way to do it — the set, the team and the talent are already paid for, so adding a film to a stills day costs far less than commissioning it separately. It has to be planned in from the start, because a day built for stills alone runs at a different pace.

Do you travel outside Dubai for editorial work?

Yes — across the UAE, and to India regularly, since I work between Dubai and Bangalore. Travel is quoted inside the same all-in figure rather than added afterwards, and a shoot that needs a specific landscape is often cheaper to fly to than to fake.
