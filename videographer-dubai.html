03

One person, or a crew

Most video here is sold at one of two extremes: a production company that arrives with eleven people and a call sheet, or someone with a camera and an afternoon. Both are right sometimes, and the gap between their quotes gets explained as a difference in quality when it is really a difference in scale.

One person is right when the subject is real and the setup is simple — a founder talking, a walkthrough, a testimonial, a social series. A small footprint is an advantage rather than a compromise: people relax faster in a room with one camera than in a room with a lighting crew, and where the whole point is that somebody sounds like themselves, that relaxation is the production value.

A crew is right when things must happen at once. Multi-camera events. A shoot where somebody watches sound while somebody else watches light. Anything with talent, a schedule and a client on set. Attempting those alone is not thrift, it is a risk, and where it shows first is the audio.

I work both ways and will tell you which your brief needs, including when the honest answer is more than one person. A quote that quietly assumes a one-person crew for a three-person job is the commonest way a cheap film becomes an expensive reshoot.

07

Video questions

What kind of video do you shoot?

Corporate and brand films, property walkthroughs, weddings, social and UGC content, and motion alongside stills campaigns. Each has its own page here with the detail, because they are priced and crewed differently — the table above points to the right one.

Do you work alone or bring a crew?

Both, depending on what the brief needs. One person suits founder interviews, walkthroughs, testimonials and social work, and people relax faster with a single camera in the room. Events, multi-camera shoots and anything with talent and a schedule need a crew, and I will say so rather than quietly under-crewing a job.

What does a videographer cost in Dubai?

It is quoted per project rather than as a day rate — shoot days, edit days, crew size and the deliverable list set it. You get one all-in figure covering the shoot and the edit, agreed before anything starts. The cost guide on this site has the market ranges if you want context first.

Can you shoot photos and video on the same day?

Yes, and it is usually the sensible way. One visit, one setup, one team — and stills and film that match because the same person lit and framed both. It costs considerably less than commissioning the two separately.

How long from shoot to final cut?

For a straightforward film, one to two weeks, with the shoot booked a week or two ahead. Scripting, talent or heavier motion graphics extend that. Rush turnarounds are possible and quoted as such.

Do you write scripts and direct?

Yes. For interview-led work there is deliberately no script — a scripted person stops sounding like a person — so I prepare questions and shape it in the edit. For anything narrative or product-led, scripting and a shot list are part of the job.

What about drone or aerial footage?

I do not fly. Aerial work is a separate specialism with its own licensing, and when a film genuinely needs it, it is arranged with a licensed operator and quoted separately. Most films do not need it — a well-shot sequence at eye level sells better than an establishing shot from above.

Do you travel outside Dubai?

Yes — across the UAE and to India regularly, since I work between Dubai and Bangalore. Travel is quoted inside the same all-in figure rather than added afterwards.
