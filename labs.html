Experiments · not linked from the site

Three ways this site gains depth

Live prototypes, built with your real frames. Tilt your phone, move your cursor, scroll — each one is a technique that could ship, applied where it would actually live.

Lab 01 · move your cursor over it — or tilt your phone

Photographs with a z-axis

The frame, the gloss and the caption sit on different depth planes, so the photo reads as an object you're holding rather than a rectangle. On phones it follows the gyroscope — the print moves as the hand does.

Where it goes: the six portfolio cards on the homepage, the hub tiles on /dubai/ and /india/, and the Journal covers.

Lab 02 · move your cursor through it

A bokeh field that answers the hand

Out-of-focus highlights drifting in three depth layers — the closest move most, the way real bokeh parallaxes when a lens moves. It's the photographic signature, made ambient.

Where it goes: behind the "Two bases, one eye" market band, the quote page, and the category-page heros — replacing flat black with atmosphere that costs almost nothing.

Lab 03 · keep scrolling — the scroll is the dolly

A dolly move through the work

Scrolling pushes the camera forward through floating frames — the one camera move every visitor gets to perform themselves. Frames drift past on both sides and fade as they pass the lens.

Where it goes: a "walk through the weddings" mode on weddings.html, or the fashion page — one act per page, never the whole site.

Scroll · the camera is yours

Any of these can ship as-is. They're plain CSS transforms and one canvas — no libraries, nothing that fights the WebGL already running on the homepage.
