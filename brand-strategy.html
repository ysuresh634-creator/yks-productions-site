Brand Strategy & Content Strategy

A brand is what people say about you when you’re not in the room. Brand strategy decides what that should be; content strategy decides how you show it every week. I do both, and because I also shoot, the plan ends in content you can actually publish.

Recent work

01

Brand strategy without the 80-page deck

Good brand strategy fits on a few pages: who you serve, the problem you solve better than anyone else, the one idea you want to own, your tone of voice, and how all of it should look on camera. Long decks tend to sit in a shared drive. Short, sharp ones get used.

My perspective comes from 8 years of making content for other people’s brands, from Vaseline (Unilever) and Marriott to KL Rahul and Cindrebay School of Design, plus unit stills on the films Soothravakyam and Baby Girl. Working on sets and campaigns teaches you quickly what a brand looks like when it’s clear, and how much time gets wasted when it isn’t.

02

Content strategy: from idea to calendar

Content strategy turns the brand into a repeatable publishing system. I define three to five content pillars, the formats each one uses (reels, carousels, long-form video, articles), the hooks and stories that fit your audience, and a realistic cadence. Then I build a calendar around shoot days so production is batched and efficient. One well-planned shoot day can cover a month of content.

03

Visual direction

Most brand guidelines stop at logos and colours. I add the things that matter on camera: lighting style, framing, colour grade reference, wardrobe and location notes, and examples of what on-brand and off-brand look like. That gives every future photographer, videographer or designer a brief they can follow.

04

Personal branding for founders

For many businesses the founder is the brand. Founder-led content on LinkedIn and Instagram builds trust faster than a logo ever will. I help founders and professionals define what they want to be known for, then plan and shoot the portraits, talking-head videos and stories that back it up. See LinkedIn marketing & personal branding.

05

Signs you need brand strategy

Your team describes the business differently every time. Your content looks different from one month to the next. Customers compare you mostly on price. You’re entering a new market, for example expanding from India to the UAE or the other way round. Any of these is a sign that positioning work will make everything else cheaper and easier.

06

How strategy shows up on camera

On a shoot, strategy becomes concrete choices: which lens, what light, how close, what the people wear, what we leave out. A brand positioned as calm and premium gets slower moves on the DJI RS3, softer light and the 85mm; a brand positioned as energetic gets the 28mm, tighter cuts and movement. Writing these choices down means every shoot, by anyone, stays on-brand.

This is also why I prefer to shoot the first content after a strategy project. It tests the strategy in the real world and gives you a reference library for everyone who comes after.

07

Content strategy for different channels

Instagram rewards entertainment and visual identity. LinkedIn rewards opinion and expertise. YouTube rewards depth. Google rewards answers. A content strategy assigns each pillar to the channels where it fits, so you’re not forcing the same post everywhere.

Common questions

What do I actually receive?

A short brand strategy document, a visual direction guide with reference frames, and a content strategy with pillars and a calendar. Optionally, the first shoot.

How long does brand strategy take?

Usually a few weeks, depending on how many people need to be involved in decisions.

Do you design logos?

No. I focus on positioning, messaging, visual direction and content. If you need a new identity, I’ll brief a designer using the strategy.

Is content strategy useful without brand strategy?

It works, but it’s weaker. If your positioning is already clear, we can start straight with content strategy.
