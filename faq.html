Gear & equipment

What's in the bag, and why each piece is there — including what I don't own.

What camera do you shoot with?

I shoot on a Sony FX3 for video and a Sony Alpha 7 IV for stills, and they work as a pair on most jobs. The FX3 is a cinema camera in a small body — it handles low light beautifully, which matters for weddings, interiors and anything after sunset. The A7 IV is my stills workhorse: fast, reliable, and files that hold up to heavy grading and large prints.

What lenses do you use?

Three Sony G Master lenses cover almost everything: the FE 24–70mm f/2.8 GM II as the do-anything zoom, the FE 35mm f/1.4 GM for environmental and story frames, and the FE 85mm f/1.4 GM for portraits and close, flattering compression. Prime lenses at f/1.4 are why the backgrounds melt the way they do.

What lighting equipment do you bring?

A Godox AD400 Pro strobe for shaping serious light on portraits, fashion and interiors, a Godox V1 Pro on-camera for run-and-gun and receptions, and an Aputure AL-MX bi-color for tight spots and accent light. I light to make a place look like the best version of itself — not to make it look lit.

How do you get such smooth camera movement?

A DJI RS 3 Pro gimbal for walking and reveal shots — that's what gives property walkthroughs their floating feel — and an Edelkrone SliderONE Pro for slow, controlled slider moves on details and interiors. Movement should have a reason; if a shot doesn't need to move, it doesn't.

Do you fly a drone or shoot aerial footage?

No. I don't fly drones and I don't hold a UAE drone licence, so I don't offer aerial footage. Everything I deliver is ground-based. In practice this matters less than people expect — for real estate, what actually sells a listing is a cinematic interior walkthrough and twilight exteriors, not a drone orbit that looks like every other listing.

Do you record proper audio, and do you back up footage on location?

Yes to both. Bad audio kills a corporate film faster than bad picture, so interviews get dedicated sound rather than camera mics. For monitoring and higher-quality recording I use an Atomos Ninja V+, and footage lives on Sony Tough CFexpress Type A cards, backed up before I leave a location. Nothing goes home on a single copy.

What happens if a camera fails mid-shoot?

I shoot with two bodies, so a failure means switching cameras rather than ending a shoot. Cards, batteries and lighting all have spares in the bag. On a wedding day especially, redundancy isn't paranoia — it's the job.

Does the gear actually matter when choosing a photographer?

Less than most people think. Gear sets a floor, not a ceiling — it decides what's possible in low light or fast movement, but it doesn't decide whether an image makes you feel something. Judge a photographer by full galleries and complete films, not by a kit list. The kit is listed here simply because clients ask. (Full breakdown on the gear page.)

Experience & film work

Where the eye came from — and why a film background changes commercial work.

How long have you been a photographer and videographer?

Over eight years behind the lens. I grew up in Alappuzha on the Kerala coast, came up through the South Indian film industry, and now work between Bangalore and Dubai shooting fashion, weddings, real estate and brand content.

What is your film industry experience?

I work as a cinematographer and on-set stills photographer in the South Indian film industry — most recently shooting the frames and behind-the-scenes for the Malayalam features Soothravakyam and Baby Girl. On a film set, the still is the film's first trailer: my job is to catch the single frame that makes you feel the whole story.

What does an on-set stills photographer actually do?

You work inside a live film set without disturbing it — silent, invisible, and always reading two moves ahead of the camera department. The stills become the posters, the press images and the marketing. It's the best training there is for weddings and events, because both are unrepeatable and nobody stops for you.

How does film experience change the way you shoot commercial work?

Film teaches you to shoot for feeling, not for documentation. You learn to read a room before the moment happens, to light so it looks motivated rather than lit, and to think in sequences instead of single shots. That's why a property film has a sense of journey, a wedding cuts like a short film, and a brand video holds attention instead of being skipped.

How many weddings and brand campaigns have you shot?

More than 100 weddings and over 200 brand campaigns across India and the UAE, alongside feature-film stills work. That volume is really about instinct: after enough weddings you stop hunting for moments and start anticipating them.

Do you shoot both photo and video yourself?

Yes — and that's deliberate. Most clients book both, and having one creative own stills and film means a single consistent look instead of two teams with different tastes blocking each other. For larger productions I bring in a trusted small team, but the eye stays the same.

What kind of work do you enjoy most?

Anything where there's a real person and real light. Property films are the most technically satisfying, weddings the most emotionally rewarding, and fashion the most creatively free. The common thread is that I'd rather make you feel a place or a person than simply record that they existed.

Working with models

Dubai, expat and Indian talent — casting, direction, lighting for skin tone, and doing it professionally.

Do you work with professional models in Dubai?

Yes — regularly. Dubai has a deep pool of professional models, and I shoot editorial, lookbook, campaign and portfolio work with them. Working with an experienced model changes the pace of a shoot: you can direct in shorthand and spend your energy on light and story rather than on posing. More on the fashion photography page.

Do you shoot expat and international models?

Yes. Dubai's model scene is genuinely international — European, Russian, Filipino, Arab, African and South Asian talent all work here — and campaigns often need a specific look or a mixed cast. I shoot across all of it and adapt lighting to skin tone rather than using one setup for everyone, which is the difference between a face that glows and a face that looks grey.

Do you shoot Indian models and South Asian talent?

Yes, extensively — in both Bangalore and Dubai. Coming from the South Indian film industry, a lot of my portrait and editorial work is with Indian models and actors, including actor portfolios. I light deliberately for deeper skin tones, which too many shoots still get wrong by underexposing or over-cooling the image.

How do you light differently for different skin tones?

By treating it as a craft decision rather than a fix in post. Deeper skin holds highlights beautifully but needs the light shaped and placed to keep dimension instead of flattening it; fairer skin needs care not to blow out. Getting exposure and white balance right in camera is what makes everyone in a mixed cast look like they were shot for the same campaign.

Can you help with casting or finding models for a shoot?

Yes. If you need talent, I can help source and coordinate models suitable for the brief and the market, quoted as an all-in managed rate within the shoot. If you already have talent, even better — I'll work to their strengths.

Can you arrange hair, makeup and styling?

For campaigns and editorial, yes — hair, makeup and styling can be arranged as part of the production and quoted inside the all-in number. For simpler shoots I'll advise on wardrobe so you don't need a full crew for something that doesn't require one.

How do you direct people who aren't professional models?

Gently and specifically. Most people freeze because they're given nothing to do, so I direct action rather than poses — where to look, what to think about, how to move — and I keep talking so nobody is stranded in silence. Founders, couples and camera-shy team members usually end up surprised by their own photos.

How do you handle image rights, releases and comfort on set?

Professionally and in writing. Usage rights are agreed up front so everybody knows where images will run, model releases are handled where required, and nobody's images are published outside what was agreed. On set, a calm and respectful environment is not a nice-to-have — it's how you get people to relax enough to be photographed well.

Shooting in Dubai

Locations, permits, light, heat — the practical stuff nobody mentions until shoot day.

Which areas of Dubai do you shoot in?

All of it — Downtown and Business Bay, Dubai Marina, JBR and the Palm, DIFC, Jumeirah, JLT, Media City, Dubai Hills, and out to Dubai Investment Park and the newer communities. I also shoot across the wider UAE including Abu Dhabi, and I travel for destination work.

Do I need a permit to shoot in Dubai?

It depends where. Private property with the owner's permission is usually straightforward, but commercial filming in public places in Dubai generally requires a permit, and individual buildings, malls, hotels and gated communities each have their own access rules. I plan for this in pre-production so the shoot day isn't the moment anyone discovers a problem.

What is the best time of day to shoot in Dubai?

Golden hour, almost always — the hour after sunrise or before sunset. The light is soft, the skyline glows, and everyone is far more comfortable than at midday. Twilight and blue-hour exteriors are the highest-converting frames in real estate, and they only exist in a short window, so shoots get planned around it rather than around convenience.

How do you handle Dubai's summer heat on a shoot?

By planning around it. In the hotter months outdoor work moves to early morning or late afternoon, we keep sessions short and shaded, use indoor and waterfront locations, and build in breaks. October to April is glorious for outdoor shoots; May to September just needs a smarter schedule.

What are your favourite locations in Dubai?

Beaches at golden hour on the Palm and JBR, the old-Dubai texture around Al Seef and Al Fahidi, the desert out toward Al Qudra for anything cinematic, and the towers of Downtown and Marina for skyline work. The best location, though, is usually the one that means something to the client rather than the one that's most photographed.

Are you based in Dubai or India?

Both. I work between Dubai and Bangalore and shoot regularly in each, plus Abu Dhabi and the wider UAE. For destination work I travel. Tell me where and when, and I'll confirm availability.

How far in advance should I book a shoot?

For weddings and dated events, as early as you can — good dates go months ahead, especially in season. For real estate, brand and portrait work, a week or two is usually comfortable, and I can often accommodate urgent listings and launches at shorter notice. It never hurts to ask about a date.

The reels that travelled

3.2M views on one reel. 1.4M on a brand reel for Leezu's. 27K likes on a single photograph. What's actually behind content that spreads — and how to get it for your brand.

What is behind your viral reels?

My top reel has passed 3.2 million views and 50,000 likes, with others at 1.5 million and 1 million. None of them were accidents. The pattern is the same each time: the first frame has to earn the second, the light has to be real rather than filtered, movement has to feel motivated, and the cut has to land on the beat. Reels don't travel because of the subject — they travel because someone couldn't scroll past the first half second.

What makes a reel actually perform?

Four things, in order. A hook in the opening frame that creates a question. Pacing that keeps giving — a new visual idea every couple of seconds. Craft the viewer can feel even if they can't name it: real light, clean movement, honest colour. And sound that matches the cut, because most of a reel's rhythm is heard, not seen. Everything else is decoration.

Does a viral reel actually bring business?

Views alone don't pay — but the right views do. A reel that reaches a million strangers is worth less than one that reaches ten thousand people who could hire you. That's why brand content should be built around who you want to reach, not around chasing a trend, and why I'd rather make a reel that converts than one that merely travels.

Can you make reels for my brand or property?

Yes — it's one of the most requested things I do. Vertical reels for Instagram, TikTok and YouTube Shorts, shot cinema-grade but cut native to the feed, for property listings, restaurants, fashion brands, clinics and founders. A single focused shoot day usually banks a month of content. See content & UGC.

How many reels do I get from one shoot day?

A focused day typically banks a month or more of content — multiple reels plus a batch of stills — because we plan the shot list around the formats before we start rather than hunting for content afterwards. The exact count depends on locations, changes and how much variety the brief needs.

Do I own the content and can I run it as paid ads?

Yes. You get full usage rights to run the content on your own channels and as paid ads, including whitelisting where relevant. The deliverables are yours to use — that's the entire point of commissioning them.

Do you also manage social media, or just shoot the content?

Both, depending on what you need. I can deliver content for your team to run, or handle the whole engine — strategy, shooting, editing, captions, posting and reporting — as a monthly retainer. Most agencies outsource the filming; I shoot it myself, which is why the content looks different. More on social media marketing.

Do you make UGC-style content that doesn't look like an ad?

Yes — creator-style content built for paid social, where a polished ad gets scrolled past but something that feels real gets watched. It's the format quietly out-converting traditional ads, and it's a different craft from a hero brand film. Many brands need both.

Do you shoot Indian weddings?

Do you shoot expat, Western and multicultural weddings?

Yes — church and civil ceremonies, beach and resort weddings, and mixed-tradition celebrations where two families do things differently. The job is the same everywhere: understand the order of the day, respect the traditions, and stay out of the way while catching what matters.

Do you offer same-day wedding edits?

Yes. A same-day edit is a short, emotional teaser cut during the day and played at the reception that same night. It's one of the most powerful things you can add to a wedding — the room reacts to it every time. The full highlight film follows within a couple of weeks. More on wedding films.

Do you shoot destination weddings?

Do you bring a second shooter?

For larger weddings, yes — a second shooter or a small team, so one camera stays on the couple while another catches reactions and the wider room. For intimate ceremonies a single shooter is often better: fewer people, more honest moments.

What happens if it rains or the schedule runs late?

Weddings always run late — that's normal and I plan for it. We agree the must-have moments up front, so if time compresses, the important frames still happen. For weather, we scout indoor and covered alternatives in advance rather than improvising on the day.

Do you shoot pre-wedding and engagement sessions?

Yes — pre-wedding and engagement shoots, both photo and film. They're also the best possible rehearsal: you get comfortable with me and with being photographed before the wedding day, and it shows in the wedding photos.

How many photos do we get, and when?

A full edited gallery rather than a curated handful — every frame worth keeping, culled and graded to one consistent look. Weddings typically land within a couple of weeks, with a preview set much sooner so you have something to share while everyone's still talking about it.

Will you tell us what to do, or just document?

Both, at the right moments. During the ceremony and celebrations I stay invisible and document. For couple portraits I direct properly — because "just be natural" is useless advice when someone's pointing a camera at you. You'll be guided without the day turning into a photoshoot.

Portraits, headshots & fashion

Editorial, personal branding and portfolio work — and how to actually prepare for it.

What should I wear for a portrait or headshot session?

Solid colours and simple shapes photograph best; busy patterns and logos pull attention from your face. Bring two or three options including one you feel genuinely confident in — confidence reads on camera more than any outfit does. For headshots, match the tone of your industry rather than dressing beyond it.

How long does a portrait session take?

A focused personal or business portrait session is usually an hour or two — enough for a couple of looks and locations without anyone getting tired, which is when faces start to stiffen. Team headshots run 5–10 minutes per person. Editorial and campaign shoots are planned as half or full days.

Do you shoot in a studio or on location?

Both. Location work gives context and natural light and usually feels more like you; studio gives complete control and a clean, consistent look. For headshots I can bring a portable studio to your office, which is the best of both.

Do you retouch photos, and how heavy is it?

Every delivered image is edited — colour, contrast and clean retouching — but the goal is that you look like the best version of yourself, not like someone else. I remove temporary things like blemishes and stray hairs, and leave the things that make you look like you.

Do you shoot actor and model portfolios?

Yes — portfolio and headshot work for actors and models in both Dubai and Bangalore, with a range of looks in one session so a portfolio shows range rather than one repeated expression. It's a natural extension of the film-stills work I do.

Do you shoot fashion lookbooks and campaigns?

Yes — lookbooks, campaigns and editorial for designers and brands, styled and lit like a campaign rather than a product listing. See fashion photography in Dubai or in Bangalore.

Can you shoot personal branding content for founders?

Yes, and it's increasingly what people book. A mix of portraits, working shots and short talking-to-camera video, banked in one session, so you have months of LinkedIn and Instagram content that looks intentional instead of improvised.

I hate being photographed. Can you work with that?

Genuinely, yes — and most people say it. The trick isn't confidence, it's direction: if you're given something to do and someone keeps talking to you, the self-consciousness fades within a few minutes. The people who dread it most are often happiest with the results.

Food, restaurants & hospitality

Menus, delivery apps and the content that fills tables.

Do you shoot restaurants and food photography?

Yes — full menu shoots, hero dishes for campaigns, delivery-app imagery for Talabat, Deliveroo and Careem, drinks and dessert, plus food reels. See food photography in Dubai.

Do you shoot at our restaurant or in a studio?

On location at your restaurant, with portable lighting, usually before service or during a quiet window so the kitchen keeps running. Studio works better for packaged food brands and product-led shoots.

Do you handle food styling?

I do light styling and plating direction on set with your chef, which is enough for most menu and social work. For large campaigns that need a dedicated food stylist, that can be arranged as part of the production.

Why does food photography need to be shot fast?

Because food dies on camera. Steam disappears, sauces dull, greens wilt and ice melts — often within a minute. The whole craft is light plus speed: getting the frame right before the dish stops looking like the dish your chef sent out.

Do you shoot hotels and holiday homes?

Yes — hospitality interiors, rooms, amenities and lifestyle content for hotels, short-stay and Airbnb operators, in both Dubai and Bangalore. Good imagery is one of the few things that reliably lifts nightly rate and occupancy.

Editing, retouching & delivery

What happens after the shoot, and what actually lands in your hands.

What is your editing and colour style?

Cinematic but honest — warm, filmic colour with real skin tones, contrast that holds shadow detail, and no heavy filters or trend grades that will look dated in a year. The aim is that an image feels the way the moment felt, not that it announces it was edited.

Can you match our brand's existing look?

Yes. If you have brand guidelines, a reference feed or a look you love, send it — I'd rather match your world than impose mine. For ongoing content clients, we lock a consistent grade so every month's batch looks like it belongs together.

Do you provide the RAW or unedited files?

Generally no — the edit is a core part of the work, so I deliver finished, colour-graded files rather than raw footage or unprocessed images. If a project genuinely needs source files, it's something to agree up front rather than after delivery.

How many photos will I receive?

A full edited gallery rather than a curated handful — every frame worth keeping, culled for quality and edited to a consistent look. The exact number depends on the length and type of shoot, which we agree in the brief so nobody's guessing.

How fast do you deliver?

Real estate photos in 24–48 hours and property films in 3–5 days. Event highlights same-day or next morning, full gallery inside 48 hours. Corporate edits in 5–7 days. Weddings within a couple of weeks, with same-day edits available for the reception. Rush turnarounds can be arranged.

How do you deliver the final files?

Through a private online gallery or drive link with high-resolution downloads, plus versions sized for web, portals and social. Films come in a hero cut with social versions alongside. Everything arrives ready to use rather than needing another round of work.

How many rounds of revisions are included?

A round of refinements on the edit is normal and included — colour tweaks, cut adjustments, small changes. The scope is agreed when we lock the project, so you're never surprised by a revision charge and I'm never caught in an endless loop.

Can I print the photos and use them commercially?

Yes. You receive high-resolution files with print rights, plus web- and social-sized versions. Commercial usage rights are agreed in the brief so you know exactly where the work can run — for brand and content jobs, that includes paid ads.

Do you keep a backup of my files?

Yes, for a period after delivery — but treat your own download as the master copy and back it up somewhere safe. Archives aren't infinite, and the safest place for your photos is with you.

Booking, quotes & working together

How pricing works, what I need from you, and what happens after you get in touch.

How much does a shoot cost?

Every brief is genuinely different, so I quote per project rather than publishing a menu that would be wrong for most people. What you get is one all-in number covering the shoot and the edit, with no hidden extras appearing later. Send the details — or use the quote assistant — and you'll have a clear number back quickly.

What drives the price of a shoot up or down?

Mostly time and deliverables. The number of hours, days or events; whether you want photo, film, or both; how many final pieces you need; whether it's one location or several; talent and styling; and how fast you need it delivered. Booking photo and film together from one visit is almost always better value than hiring twice.

What do you need from me to give a quote?

Four things: what you're shooting, where, roughly when, and what you need to end up with. That's enough for a real number. Anything else — references, moodboards, a shot list — helps but isn't required to start.

How do I get a quote?

Use the quote assistant — it asks the right questions in about a minute and sends me a complete brief — or message me directly on WhatsApp. Either way you get availability and an all-in quote back, usually within a few hours.

Do you provide a contract, and how do bookings work?

Yes. Once we agree the brief and the quote, the booking is confirmed in writing covering dates, deliverables, usage rights and terms, so both sides know exactly what was agreed. Booking terms are laid out clearly at that point rather than buried in small print.

Can I be involved in planning the shoot?

As much as you'd like. Some clients send a detailed shot list and moodboard; others say "make it look great" and leave me to it. Both work — what doesn't work is discovering on the day that we had different pictures in our heads, so we align before we shoot.

What if we need to reschedule?

Do you travel outside Dubai and Bangalore?

Choosing a photographer

Honest advice for hiring anyone — including how to tell if it shouldn't be me.

What makes you different from a production agency?

Most agencies sell strategy and then outsource the camera, so the content comes back looking like everyone else's. I'm film-trained and shoot it myself — you deal with the person actually holding the camera, decisions happen in one head instead of three, and the look stays consistent. Agency-quality output at creator pace and cost.

Why shouldn't I just hire the cheapest option?

Because most of what you're buying can't be seen in a quote. A wedding happens once and can't be re-shot; a listing worth millions is marketed by the video attached to it. Cheap usually means slower delivery, flatter light, and content that fills a slot without doing a job. Judge on full galleries and complete films first, then compare price.

How do I judge whether a photographer is actually good?

Ask for a complete gallery and a full film, not a highlight clip — anyone can cut six good seconds. Check consistency across a whole job, look at whether verticals are straight and skin tones look real, and see whether they've shot your kind of work before.
