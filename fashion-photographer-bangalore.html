The maths of a catalogue day

Most brands in this city are shooting to sell online, and the only question that really matters is how much of the range gets through in a day.

What tends to slow a catalogue shoot down, and what a day realistically covers.
Set-upWhat it involvesPace
Flat lay / ghost mannequinGarment only, lit once and shot repeatedlyFastest by a wide margin. A large part of a range can clear in a day
On-model, one lookSame styling, same light, straight through the railQuick, and the most common way a D2C range is shot
On-model, styled changesHair, makeup or accessories changing between piecesRoughly half the pace. The changes cost the time, not the photography
Ethnic & bridalDrapes, pleats, heavy fabrics, jewellerySlowest of all — a single saree can take as long as several simpler garments

The practical consequence: if the range is large, group it by set-up rather than by collection. Shooting everything that needs one lighting state before moving to the next is the difference between a comfortable day and an overrun. It is also why an accurate answer to “how many pieces?” is worth more in your first message than any description of the mood.

04

Ethnic, bridal and heavy fabric

A large share of the fashion work in Bangalore is ethnic wear, and it is genuinely harder to photograph than western clothing. Silk behaves differently under light — it throws specular highlights that blow out if the light is hard and goes lifeless if the light is too soft. Zari and heavy embroidery need a light that rakes across them or the work simply vanishes, which for a garment whose value is the work is the worst possible outcome. Deep saturated colours, particularly reds, clip before the rest of the frame does and need watching on the day rather than fixing afterwards.

Then there is drape. A saree or a lehenga is not really photographed as a flat object; it is arranged, and the arranging is most of the job. That is why these shoots run slower and why somebody whose hands know how to set a pleat is worth more on set than an extra light.

None of this is exotic knowledge, but it is the reason ethnic ranges shot by someone who mostly does western catalogue work come back looking flat. If your range is heavy on silk, embroidery or bridal, say so at the brief — it changes the lighting plan, the pace and how the day is built.

08

Fashion shoot questions

Do you shoot lookbooks and catalogues for D2C brands?

Yes — it is the most common fashion work in Bangalore. A catalogue is shot for clarity and consistency so a customer can judge cut, drape and colour, which is a different discipline from editorial and is priced on volume rather than on concept.

How many garments can we shoot in a day?

It depends entirely on the set-up rather than on the photography. Flat lay and mannequin move fastest, on-model with a single styling is quick, and anything with hair, makeup or accessory changes between pieces runs at roughly half that pace. Ethnic and bridal is slowest, because draping is most of the work.

Do you shoot ethnic and bridal wear?

Yes, and it needs a different lighting plan from western clothing. Silk throws highlights that blow out under hard light, zari and embroidery disappear unless the light rakes across them, and deep reds clip before the rest of the frame. If your range is heavy on those, say so at the brief — it changes the pace and how the day is built.

Can you arrange models, hair and makeup?

Yes. Casting, hair and makeup and styling can all be handled as part of the shoot and quoted inside the one figure, so you are not running three separate searches. If you already have a model booked or a team you like, I work with them instead.

What does a fashion shoot in Bangalore cost?

It is quoted per project rather than from a rate card, because a forty-piece catalogue day and a six-frame editorial are different products. Tell me the number of pieces, whether it is on-model, and what team you need, and you will get one all-in figure covering the shoot and the edit.

Studio or location?

Both, and for catalogue work a controlled studio is usually right — consistency across the range matters more than atmosphere, and a customer comparing two products should not be distracted by two different backgrounds. Editorial and campaign work is where location earns its cost.

How soon do we get the images?

For catalogue work a first batch usually lands within a few days of the shoot, with the full retouched set following depending on volume. Editorial work is slower per frame because the retouching is done individually rather than in a batch.

Do you shoot model portfolios?

Yes. A portfolio is the opposite discipline to a campaign — clean, honest and varied, because an agency needs to see range and the real face rather than the styling. It is worth being clear which of the two you want, because they do not look alike.

Do you travel outside Bangalore?

Yes — across India, and to Dubai regularly, since I work between the two. Travel is quoted inside the same all-in number rather than added afterwards.
