How working together actually works

Three things run at the same time, from the day you sign.

This page is the honest summary. What follows is the same thing written properly, so that if anything ever goes wrong — a brand vanishes, a payment stalls, someone disputes what was agreed — there is a document with both our names on it. Every section in it also carries a plain-English note, so you never have to decode a clause to know where you stand.

1

You go into a talent pool built to actually be found

Your profile sits on a roster that is properly optimised for search, so when a brand or agency goes looking for someone in your category, you come up. Most agencies never bother doing this part — they collect creators and wait. Your contact details stay private; enquiries come through me.

2

Your profile gets pitched out, actively

I run an AI-assisted outreach engine that puts your profile in front of relevant brands — the right categories, the right people, continuously. That is the difference between waiting for enquiries and going and getting them, at a volume no one pitching by hand can match. I will never inflate your numbers, your rates, or your past work to get a meeting.

3

Whatever already comes to you, I handle too — if you want me to

The enquiries you are already getting on your own: send them to me and I will close them. Terms, rate, scope, usage, the actual shoot happening properly, the invoice, the follow-up, and the money landing in your account on time. Nothing sits in your DMs for three weeks. This is your choice per enquiry — anything you'd rather handle yourself, you handle yourself, and I charge nothing on it.

And just as importantly — what this is not
Not exclusive. You can work with other agencies, other managers, and direct with brands. You are not locked to me.
You pay me nothing. No joining fee, no monthly fee, no commission, no cut of your earnings. I'm paid by the brand, never by you.
Not a guarantee of work. I can promise effort and process. Nobody can honestly promise bookings, and I won't.
No obligation to say yes. Any brief, any category, any brand — you can decline it with no penalty and no explanation owed.
I don't own your content. Your videos, your photos, your accounts stay yours. Brands get only the usage you agreed to, for the time you agreed to.
Not a lock-in. Thirty days' notice in writing and we're done, cleanly, with anything already booked seen through.
On the money, plainly — you pay me nothing. Ever.
Zero. No joining fee, no monthly fee, no commission, no cut of what you earn — not on work I bring you, and not on your own enquiries either. You tell me the number you want in your hand; that exact number is what reaches your account. Whatever I negotiate above it is what the brand pays me, and if I can't get anything above it, you're still paid in full and I earn nothing that time.

6Money — and Why None of It Comes Out of the Creator's Pocket

In plain words

Zero, on every Campaign. The Creator pays YKS nothing on any Managed Deal. No commission is charged, no percentage is taken, and nothing is deducted from the Creator Fee. This applies equally to Sourced Deals and to Inbound Deals. YKS is remunerated only by the Brand, through the YKS Margin.

In plain words

7.1
Payment route. The Parties elect in Schedule A one of the two routes below. The route may be varied for an individual Campaign by written agreement.
7.2
Route 1 — collection by YKS. The Brand pays YKS the Gross Fee. YKS pays the Creator the full Creator Fee by bank transfer within [seven (7)] business days of receiving cleared funds, and will send a written statement showing the amount received attributable to the Creator, any statutory deduction under Clause 7.7 with its reason, and the amount remitted. No other deduction appears on that statement, because there is none.
7.3
Route 2 — split payment. The Brand pays the Creator Fee directly to the Creator, and pays the YKS Margin directly to YKS against YKS's own invoice to the Brand. The Creator will notify YKS within [three (3)] business days of receiving their funds. The Creator never invoices, collects or remits YKS's Margin, and owes YKS nothing if the Brand fails to pay YKS's share.
7.4
Client money. Where Route 1 applies, amounts received from a Brand that are attributable to the Creator are received by YKS for the Creator's account, are not YKS's income, will be held for the Creator, and will not be applied to any other purpose.

Brand default. YKS will pursue overdue Brand payments diligently, including written demand and escalation. YKS is not, however, a guarantor of any Brand's payment and is not obliged to pay the Creator for a Campaign the Brand has not paid for, save under Clause 7.6.
7.6
Guaranteed pay dates. Where YKS expressly gives the Creator a guaranteed pay date in writing for a Campaign, YKS will pay the Creator on that date whether or not the Brand has paid, and will be subrogated to the Creator's claim against the Brand for that amount. Taxes. Each Party is responsible for its own taxes. ⚠ SETTLE BEFORE SIGNING — Route 1 has a GST consequence. If Brand money for Campaigns flows through the YKS account, the full Gross Fee — not just YKS's margin — may count toward YKS's aggregate turnover for the ₹20 lakh GST registration threshold, unless YKS is properly documented as a pure agent under Rule 33 of the CGST Rules, 2017. 9Advertising Law, Disclosure and Endorsement

In plain words

This section protects you more than it protects me. Under Indian law the penalties for a misleading endorsement can land on you, personally — not just on the brand. So: always label the ad properly, never endorse something you haven't actually used, and never repeat a claim you think is rubbish. If a brand pushes, that's my conversation to have, not yours.

9.1
Disclosure is mandatory. Every Campaign post is an advertisement and must carry a disclosure label in accordance with the ASCI Guidelines for Influencer Advertising in Digital Media. The label must be upfront and prominent, on the content itself and not only in a comment, bio or link; in the same language as the post; superimposed and legible for long enough to be noticed in video; and repeated periodically in a live stream. YKS will pass on the Brand's disclosure requirements; the Creator is responsible for the label actually appearing.
9.2
Endorser due diligence. Under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019 and the Guidelines for Prevention of Misleading Advertisements and Endorsements for Misleading Advertisements, 2022, an endorser must have actually used or experienced the product or service, and must have exercised due diligence on the claims made. The Creator will not endorse a product they have not used, and will not repeat a claim they have reason to believe is untrue. The Creator acknowledges that penalties under that Act can attach to the endorser personally, including a ban on making endorsements.
9.3
Right to refuse a claim. The Creator may refuse to say anything they believe to be false, misleading or unsafe, and may require the Brand to substantiate a claim, without that refusal being a breach of this Agreement or of the Campaign.
9.4
Restricted sectors. Campaigns in regulated or restricted categories — including alcohol and surrogate advertising, tobacco, real-money gaming and betting, health, medical, nutraceutical, financial and virtual-digital-asset products — carry additional statutory and ASCI requirements. YKS will not pitch the Creator into these categories without asking first, and the Creator may decline any of them outright in Schedule A.
9.5
Platform rules. The Creator will use the platform's own paid-partnership or branded-content tool where the platform requires it, in addition to the disclosure under Clause 9.1.
⚠ Lawyer to review: ASCI guidance and the CCPA endorsement guidelines are updated periodically, and the influencer-specific due-diligence and health/finance-sector rules have tightened since 2023. Have current text confirmed before this is issued, and re-check annually.

16Indemnity and Liability
