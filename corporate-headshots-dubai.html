04

The problem that shows up six months later

Most companies do this once, get a good matched set, and then quietly ruin it. Someone joins in March, nobody wants to book a photographer for one person, so their phone selfie goes on the team page next to twenty properly lit portraits. Do that four more times and the page looks worse than it did before the shoot, because now the inconsistency looks like carelessness rather than circumstance.

It is avoidable, and the answer is boring: the lighting setup, background, crop and retouching recipe from your session are written down and kept, so a new joiner can be photographed months later and dropped into the existing set without anyone being able to tell which ones came later. In practice that means a short visit once you have a few new faces to do at once, rather than an awkward one-person booking every time.

Worth deciding before the first session rather than after, because it slightly changes how the original is shot — a setup that can be rebuilt exactly is a setup with fewer clever, unrepeatable choices in it.

05

Why it works

The hard part of a headshot isn't the camera — it's getting a busy, slightly self-conscious professional to relax in ninety seconds. That's a directing skill, and it's where a film background shows: I direct posture, angle and expression so people who "hate photos" come out looking like the best, most confident version of themselves. Same light and crop across the team means it all matches — one company, one standard.

09

Headshot questions

Do you come to our office for headshots?

Yes. I bring a portable studio setup to your office in Dubai and shoot the whole team on site against a clean backdrop, so nobody loses half a day travelling. I can also shoot in a studio or use your branded environment if you'd prefer an on-brand background.

How much space do you need?

Roughly three metres of clear floor and a power socket is enough. A meeting room with the table pushed aside works perfectly well — the room does not need to be impressive, because you will not be able to see it in the photographs.

How long does each person take?

Around 5 to 10 minutes per person once the setup is running — I direct posture and expression so even camera-shy team members come out looking confident. A team of twenty is comfortably done in a few hours.

Will the whole team's headshots match?

Yes — that's the point. Same light, same backdrop, same crop and the same retouching style across everyone, so your website and LinkedIn look like one company, not twenty different selfies.

What happens when someone joins later?

The setup from your session is recorded — lighting, background, crop and retouching — so a new joiner can be photographed months afterwards and dropped into the existing set without looking out of place. Usually that means a short visit once a few new faces have accumulated, rather than a booking for one person.

What should the team wear?

Send round one line asking for solid mid-tones and no busy patterns, and that is most of it. If your company has a dress code the photographs should match it, because a headshot that looks nothing like how someone turns up to work is a strange thing to put on a website.

Some of our team hate having their photo taken. Is that a problem?

It is the normal case rather than the exception, and handling it is most of the job. Ten minutes and being given something specific to do turns almost anyone around — the people who warn me in advance are very often the ones happiest with the result.

How fast is delivery, and is retouching included?

Edited, retouched headshots are typically delivered within 2 to 3 days, each cleanly retouched and cropped for web and LinkedIn. Rush delivery is available when you need them for a launch or a new hire.
