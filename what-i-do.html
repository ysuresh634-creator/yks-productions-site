The honest bit

Why me — not an agency, a freelancer, or a full-time hire?

Fair question — those are your real options. So instead of pretending the doubt isn't sitting in your head, let me answer it straight.

I've been burned before.

Most founders I talk to have paid an agency, got a beautiful deck and three months of "brand building", and can't point to one number that moved. I can't undo that — but I can be the opposite of it. You see every rupee from day one, and there's no lock-in — I have to re-earn the retainer every single month. Firing me is easy. That's on purpose.

Will my brand actually get your attention?

An agency runs thirty accounts; the intern gets yours. I take two brands a quarter — that's the whole roster. The person who plans your strategy is the person behind the camera, the person writing your captions, and the person reading your cost-per-lead at midnight. Same person. Me.

Who do I hold accountable when something flops?

When the shoot, the posts, the ads and the leads are four different vendors, every miss is somebody else's fault. Here, there's nobody to pass it to. If it worked, that was me. If it flopped, that's also me — and you'll hear it from me first, with what I'm changing. One person, answerable for the whole thing.

I don't have time to manage another person.

You brief once. I come back with the plan, then the work, then the numbers. No chasing, no "any update?" texts, no meetings that should have been a message. The report is a live link you open whenever you're curious — 2pm or 2am. Your time goes back to running the company.

Does the math actually make sense?

A five-person team runs about ₹35L a year before you spend a rupee on ads. A decent agency retainer isn't far behind — and you still have to manage them. I cost less than either and ship more than both, because AI handles my grunt work and the craft stays human. And if the numbers ever stop making sense — see the first answer. Leaving is easy.
