Bengaluru runs on startups, brands and ideas — and most of their video looks like a template: flat lighting, stock music, a logo at the end. It ticks a box; it doesn't move anyone. I come at video from a film background, shooting cinematography and stills for the South Indian film industry, so a YKS film is lit, paced and graded the way a scene is — to make people feel something and keep watching.

That's what works for the founders, D2C brands and event teams competing for attention here. Whether it's a launch film, a founder story, a product video or a 30-second reel, the goal is the same: video that looks far bigger than the budget behind it, and earns the watch and the enquiry.
