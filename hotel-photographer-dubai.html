Dubai

What’s different here

The UAE brief usually isn't one property, it's one property in four registers — the brand site, the OTA listing, the trade deck and the social feed, each wanting a different crop and a different energy from the same suite. Planning the shot list around all four from the start is the difference between one shoot and three. Golden hour here is short and the light is hard; the schedule gets built around that, not around the hotel's convenience.

How a hotel shoot works

From recce to delivery

01. Brief and recce

You send room categories, brand guidelines, and where the pictures have to run: brand site, OTAs, trade deck, social. I walk the property (or review floor plans and current photos if we cannot recce) and build a shot list against all four, not just the suite that photographs easily.

02. A schedule that fits occupancy

Show rooms and turnover rooms go first. Exteriors and views are timed to the hour each elevation actually looks like itself. F&B is shot when the room is dressed, not when it happens to be empty. You get a running order the duty manager can work with, not a rigid call sheet that collapses the first time a guest checks out late.

03. Stills and motion together

Once a suite is lit, I shoot the stills and the motion from the same setup. That is what keeps the brand site, the property film and the reels looking like one hotel. Social cuts are planned on the day rather than salvaged from the film afterwards.

04. Grade and delivery

You get a graded set cropped for the channels we planned for, plus the property film and vertical reels if they were in the brief. Usage covers your own marketing (brand site, OTAs, trade, print, paid social) with no yearly relicensing.

Across the city

Hotel photography across Dubai

City hotels around Downtown and Business Bay, waterfront properties in Dubai Marina and JBR, resort-scale buildings on the Palm, business hotels near DIFC, beach hotels along Jumeirah, and airport-side properties in Deira and near DXB: the brief changes with the address. A marina tower is about the view and the room width. A Palm resort is about grounds, arrival and how far the villas sit from each other. A Downtown hotel is often about the skyline at a specific hour.

I plan around those differences, and around Dubai light, which is hard and short at both ends of the day. In summer the exterior work moves to early morning and the last hour before dark; the middle of the day goes to rooms, spa and F&B. That is a scheduling problem, not a reason to wait until November.

What shapes the quote

How to choose a hotel photographer, and what moves the number

Ask to see a full set from one room category, not only hero frames. A booking decision is made by comparing two room types; if the deluxe and the suite were lit differently, the guest is comparing the photography, not the rooms. Ask whether stills and film come from the same visit, who will actually be on the property (it will be me), and whether the shot list is built for OTAs and the brand site together.

Room categories

Four room types, a restaurant and a pool is a different day from two suites and a lobby. Category count moves the quote more than the star rating does.

How much of the property

Rooms only is one job. Adding F&B, spa, gym, arrival, ballrooms and a property film is another. Say what has to be in the set before I quote, so we are not discovering it on site.

Stills, film, or both

Motion from the same lighting setup adds hours, not a second production. If you know you need a property film and reels, put them in the brief. Adding them later usually means lighting the same suite twice.

Occupancy and season

An occupied floor is normal and doable; it is simply slower. Summer exteriors need a split day. Neither is a reason to invent extras afterwards. I quote one all-in number covering the shoot, the grade and delivery.

Questions

The ones hotels actually ask

Do you shoot stills and video on the same visit?

Yes, and it is usually the whole reason to book me rather than two suppliers. The lighting setup for a suite is most of the work; shooting motion from the same setup costs hours, not days, and everything matches afterwards because it came from one camera and one grade.

How long does a property take?

It depends on room categories more than on size. A city hotel with four room types, a restaurant and a pool is usually a full day plus an early start. A resort with villas spread over acres is two to three, because you are chasing the same hour of light across a lot of ground.

Can we use the images on Booking.com and Expedia?

Yes. You get full usage for your own marketing — brand site, OTA listings, trade decks, print, paid social, in perpetuity. I do not licence hospitality work by the year or by the channel; you paid for the shoot, the pictures are yours.

Do you work with the hotel’s brand guidelines?

Always, and it is easier when I get them early. Group properties usually arrive with a photography standard from head office covering angles, styling and grade. Send it before the recce and the shot list gets built against it, rather than reshot afterwards.

What does it cost?

One all-in number per property, quoted after a short call about categories, dates and where the images need to run. It covers the shoot, the grade and delivery — no per-image fees and no separate licensing line later.

Can you work around occupancy? We cannot empty the floor.

That is the normal condition, not a complication. Show apartments and turnover rooms get shot first, the rest fits around housekeeping, and I work with your duty manager on a running order rather than a fixed schedule. It is slower than an empty property and entirely doable.

Do you shoot in summer?

Yes, with the schedule inverted. From roughly June to September everything exterior happens either side of the heat — early morning and the last hour before dark — and the middle of the day goes to rooms, spa and F&B. It is a planning problem, not a reason to wait until November.

We need assets for the brand site, the OTAs and the trade deck. Same shoot?

Same shoot, and it should be planned that way from the start. Those four channels want different crops and different energy from the same suite, so the shot list gets built against all of them up front. Deciding afterwards is how a property ends up paying for the same room twice.
